Standard product or custom build?

Use the smallest answer that actually solves the problem.

If Quote Builder or Business Office already handles the need, Highway 38 can recommend the standard product instead of charging for unnecessary custom development. When the workflow, customer experience, field process, integration, or device requirement is unique, the work can be scoped as a custom digital service.

Typical reasons to go custom

A unique customer or employee workflow
A specialized calculator, configurator, or quoting tool
Existing systems that must work together
Field capture requiring phone hardware or offline behavior
A branded portal or application that customers use directly
How a custom project moves

Scope first. Build second. Verify before launch.

01

Define the result

Users, jobs-to-be-done, current process, must-keep systems, data, integrations, devices, risks, and the result that would make the project worthwhile.

02

Confirm scope and price

Pages, screens, workflows, roles, integrations, migration, launch target, milestones, third-party costs, ownership, support, and acceptance criteria are written down before the build begins.

03

Build and test

The system is built against the approved scope with functional, mobile/desktop, security, data, and workflow checks appropriate to the project.

04

Launch and hand off

Production deployment, account ownership, source/code handoff when included, documentation, training, support responsibilities, and the next review point are confirmed.

Important boundaries

Custom does not mean undefined.

App-store approval, third-party subscriptions, payment processing, messaging, maps, hosting, domains, external APIs, data migration, and other connected services can carry separate provider requirements or costs. Those items are identified during scoping rather than silently bundled into an open-ended promise.

Controlled actions remain controlled

AI and automation can prepare, organize, route, and recommend. Customer messages, payments, purchases, scheduling commitments, approvals, and other external business actions are enabled only when the approved project explicitly requires and verifies them.
