Speeds & Feeds

Surface finish (milling)

Theoretical scallop height Rt = feed² ÷ (8 × nose radius) — the standard geometry, same as turning. Ra is shown as the customary Rt ÷ 4 approximation. Real finish depends on runout, rigidity, and material.

Finish planner (turning)

Rt is the theoretical peak-to-valley scallop height (feed² ÷ (8 × nose radius)); for a target Ra, plan on Rt ≈ 4 × Ra. Workbook note: finish calcs assume 80° diamond / trigon inserts; 55° & 35° inserts run rougher.

Tool life / speed adjustment

Speed adjustment uses Taylor's tool-life equation (V × Tn = C, n = 0.25 for carbide — the Machinery's Handbook standard relation) from the 15-minute base life.

Gear Basics

Standard full-depth involute gear relations — addendum 1 ÷ DP, dedendum 1.25 ÷ DP (module gears: 1 × and 1.25 × module).

For a standard full-depth gear, OD = (teeth + 2) ÷ DP — so a measured OD and a tooth count identify the pitch. Worn or non-standard gears can read off.

Press Force

F = cut length × thickness × shear strength, from the workbook's press notes. Ejection ≈ 10% of cutting force.

Spring deflection (workbook rule)

Y = (3 to 4) × (t + 2) mm, from the workbook's press notes.

Welding

Shop welding math and starting points: fillet throats, heat input, MIG and TIG starter settings, stick electrodes, and the basic weld symbols. Settings here get you close — your machine's door chart and a test bead on scrap win.

Fillet weld — leg ⇄ throat

For an equal-leg fillet with a flat face, the theoretical throat is the leg × 0.7071 — the shortest distance from the root to the face, and the throat is the dimension weld strength is figured from.

Heat input

Gross heat input H = volts × amps × 60 ÷ travel speed. This is arc energy before any arc-efficiency factor — a welding code that works in effective heat input applies its own factor.

TIG amps — rule of thumb

The shop rule of thumb is roughly 1 amp per 0.001 in of thickness for steel. Stainless runs a little less; aluminum on AC needs more to get a puddle started. Tungsten size, joint fit-up, and position all move the real setting — start here and adjust on the puddle.

Weld symbols (AWS A2.4 basics)

Every symbol hangs on a horizontal reference line, and the arrow points at the joint. A symbol below the line means weld the arrow side — the side the arrow touches. A symbol above the line means the other side. Symbols on both sides mean weld both sides, and a tail on the end of the line carries extra notes — process, electrode, or spec.

Center drills — Type A (60°) & spot drills

SizeDrill ØBody ØPoint length

Standard combined drill & countersink sizes (ASA B5.20, plain Type A). Point length is the 60° cone from drill Ø to body Ø — drill that deep to seat the full cone. Bell type (Type B, 100° guard) uses the same drill and body sizes with a rounded protective guard around the point. Overall length varies by maker.

Spot drills: common sizes run 1/8″–1″ in 90°, 118°, and 120° points. Spot with an angle equal to or wider than the drill's point (120° spot for 118° drills, 140° for 135° split points), and spot to at least the chamfer diameter you want — a 90° spot drill both spots and chamfers in one pass.

Keyways & keyseats — parallel keys (ASME B17.1)

Shaft Ø (in)Key W × HShaft keyseat depthHub keyway depth

Standard parallel key sizes per ASME B17.1, for shafts 1/4″–3″. Nominal depth is half the key height on both sides; the standard controls the measured dimension across the bottom of the keyseat (shaft: D − depth; hub: bore Ø + depth) and the fit class — check B17.1 for production tolerances.

O-ring sizes — AS568 dash numbers

DashIDODCross-section

Nominal inside diameter, outside diameter, and cross-section in inches, per AS568. Coverage: -001 through -028, the full -100 series (-102 to -178), and -201 through -250 of the -200 series — the sizes a machine shop actually grabs. The -300 series uses a 0.210″ cross-section and the -400 series 0.275″.
