# Ground-Up New-House Demonstration — Source of Truth

## Controlling scope

This Highway 38 Universal Quote Builder demonstration is a **ground-up new-home construction project on a vacant or undeveloped lot**.

The project begins with:

1. Surveying, staking, utility locating, erosion control, and a stabilized construction entrance.
2. Lot clearing, grubbing, stump removal, tree protection, topsoil stripping, and stockpiling.
3. Rough grading, house-pad preparation, excavation, foundation work, and utility trenching.

It continues through foundation, framing, dry-in, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, insulation, drywall, interior finishes, exterior flatwork, final grading, landscaping, testing, inspections, and closeout.

## Prohibited scope drift

Do not describe or redesign this demonstration as any of the following:

- House renovation
- Remodel
- Existing-house demolition package
- Property-improvement-only project
- Isolated deck, kitchen, or trade project

Those may exist as separate Highway 38 examples, but they are not the scope of this whole-house demonstration.

## Drawing coordination rule

All architectural and trade drawings must use the same coordinated building footprint, walls, rooms, doors, windows, stairs, porch, deck, foundation, grade, and site orientation.

No fixture, cabinet, register, return, duct, pipe, plumbing fixture, receptacle, switch, light, panel, utility, porch component, deck component, chimney, stair, or callout may appear as an unexplained floating object.

Every object must be visibly connected or anchored to at least one of the following:

- Wall or partition
- Floor or ceiling plane
- Roof plane
- Foundation or footing
- Finished or proposed grade
- Cabinet or equipment assembly
- Utility or trade route
- Dimension, grid, section, detail, or keyed-note reference

## Required project sequence

The customer proposal and sub-quotes must follow a ground-up construction sequence:

1. Preconstruction, survey, design, and permits
2. Lot clearing, erosion control, and temporary access
3. Excavation, house pad, site utilities, and rough grading
4. Footings, foundation, waterproofing, and slabs
5. Structural framing, sheathing, trusses, and stairs
6. Roofing, siding, windows, and exterior doors
7. Plumbing
8. Electrical
9. HVAC and ventilation
10. Insulation, air sealing, drywall, and interior prime
11. Cabinets, countertops, interior doors, and millwork
12. Flooring, tile, painting, and finish hardware
13. Porches, deck, driveway, concrete, and exterior flatwork
14. Final grading, landscaping, testing, inspections, and closeout

## Release rule

A release must fail when:

- Renovation or remodeling wording reappears in the whole-house package.
- Trade drawings use blank grids instead of the coordinated architectural underlay.
- Objects are visibly detached from the building, site, assembly, or trade route.
- Lot clearing and the ground-up construction sequence are missing.
- The rendered sheets have overlapping labels, clipped notes, invalid SVG/XML, or title-block collisions.
