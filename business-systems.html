What happens after a customer says yes?

These demonstrations use familiar project types to show how the Business Office keeps the customer, job, field work, money, approvals, and proof connected from intake through closeout.

Contractor workflow

Deck project

A signed quote becomes an organized job instead of another folder, text thread, and pile of receipts.

  1. Customer and property stay connectedThe original request, site photos, measurements, quote, messages, and approval remain attached to one record.
  2. Approved quote becomes the jobScope, price, tasks, materials, exclusions, and required proof move into the work record without retyping.
  3. Materials and field work are controlledPurchases are linked to the job, tasks are assigned, and the crew records progress photos and completion checks.
  4. Changes require approvalA railing upgrade or hidden repair is documented, priced, and approved before it changes the job total.
  5. Closeout finishes the business recordThe invoice, payment, final photos, warranty information, and job profitability are preserved together.

Owner result: The office can see what is approved, what is blocked, what has been spent, what is still owed, and whether the job made money.

Service workflow

Irrigation installation

The Business Office coordinates the site visit, materials, crew work, proof, billing, and future service opportunity.

  1. Request enters TodayThe new lead appears with follow-up timing, property information, photos, and missing measurements clearly flagged.
  2. Site details become usable recordsZones, heads, controller information, utilities, measurements, assumptions, and customer choices stay with the project.
  3. Quote approval triggers the next stepsThe job is scheduled, tasks are assigned, materials are reserved, and the deposit status is visible.
  4. Purchases and proof follow the workVendor receipts, actual costs, trench photos, pressure checks, controller setup, and customer signoff are captured.
  5. The system creates future valueSeasonal startup, winterization, warranty, and maintenance follow-ups become scheduled opportunities instead of forgotten notes.

Owner result: Nothing important depends on memory—site information, field proof, costs, customer promises, and repeat-work timing remain searchable.

Complex project workflow

Kitchen remodel

A longer project stays controlled while selections, vendors, allowances, changes, payments, and documents keep moving.

  1. Scope and selections are organizedPlans, photos, cabinet choices, allowances, exclusions, customer decisions, and open questions are tracked in one job.
  2. Vendor and subcontractor records connectQuotes, purchase orders, insurance documents, receipts, scheduled work, and contact history remain tied to the project.
  3. Today surfaces the blockersThe owner sees pending approvals, overdue selections, material delays, schedule conflicts, and tasks that need attention now.
  4. Changes and staged billing stay controlledEach change order records the reason, amount, customer approval, schedule effect, invoice stage, and payment status.
  5. Closeout creates a complete handoffFinal proof, inspection records, manuals, warranties, lien documents, final invoice, and customer follow-up form the permanent record.

Owner result: The system shows the operational and financial condition of the project before a missed approval, cost overrun, or unpaid invoice becomes a surprise.
