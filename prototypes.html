How it works

Three steps. No ballparks.

01

Send the rough version

A sketch, a drawing, a photo, measurements, or the broken part itself. Tell us what it has to do and how many you need.

02

Get a written quote

Every job is quoted in writing before work starts: what we’ll make, the material, the price, and the timing. No ballparks dressed up as prices.

03

Approve it — we make it

You approve the quote, we make the part, and you get it in hand. If the prototype proves out, we can talk about a short run — or the machine it points to.

Straight answers

If it shouldn’t be made that way, we’ll tell you.

A printed part that should be metal. A sign material that won’t survive a Minnesota winter. We’d rather flag it before you spend money than after. Highway 38 was built by a machinist — this side of the shop is home turf.

Prototype first, machine second

A prototype that proves out can lead to buying the machine. That path — fixtures, tooling, process planning — is the rest of the machine side.
